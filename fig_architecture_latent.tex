\caption{\textbf{The per-fish latent variant: how individuality enters, and where it does not.}
The chain of Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} is unchanged --- the within-bout conditioners $a$ and
$c$ still run turn $\to$ travel $\to$ interval and are omitted here for clarity. A single
$m=2$ latent $z$ per fish enters through three \emph{level-only} loadings (violet), each a
bias-free linear map from $z$ to one scalar. On the turn head it shifts the forward
component's logit alone, so a fish can be more or less likely to swim straight without
altering the shape of its turn distribution. On the travel and interval heads it adds a
common offset to \emph{every} mixture component's log-scale location, which multiplies the
typical step and pause by $e^{\lambda^{\top} z}$ while leaving the mixture weights and spreads
intact. The dashed grey path, which would let $z$ enter the shared body, is disabled: with it
on, 90\,\% of the latent's effect on the turn head flowed through it rather than through the
level loadings, the pooled marginals degraded, and the arm finished worse than its anchor.
Because $z$ is a latent with a prior rather than a per-fish parameter, individuality is both
\emph{fitted} and \emph{scored}: the reported likelihood marginalises $z$ out, so it is
comparable to a pooled model on the same held-out fish.}
