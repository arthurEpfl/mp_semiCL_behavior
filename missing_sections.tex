\subsection{The angular reference frame}

\paragraph{Idea.} Every egocentric covariate in the feature vector --- the bearing to the dish
centre $\psi$, the bearing to the stimulus $\phi$ --- is an angle measured relative to \emph{some}
forward direction of the fish. Two candidates exist in the data: the tracked body orientation
$f_{\text{ori}}$, and the travel heading $\theta$ actually realised between bout onsets. They are
not the same measurement. Their median disagreement is $40.6^\circ$, and on $10.3\%$ of bouts they
differ by more than $90^\circ$ --- uncorrected body-axis flips, where the tracker has the fish
pointing backwards.

This matters because the two frames are used \emph{asymmetrically}. Training reads
$f_{\text{ori}}$ from the record; the free-running rollout has no body axis at all and must compute
its bearings from its own accumulated heading, which is a travel direction. So the model is fitted
on a noisy version of a covariate and then queried with the clean one. That is
errors-in-variables: the fitted response to $\psi$ is attenuated toward zero, and it is attenuated
in precisely the channel the border block exists to carry. The measured signature is a wall
response that is nearly flat where the real one is steep (\S\ref{sub:borderbasis}), and it is why
a generation-time containment patch was needed at all.

\paragraph{How.} Set \texttt{psi\_ref='travel'} (and, optionally, \texttt{phi\_ref='travel'} for
the stimulus bearing). The reference becomes the \emph{previous} travel heading
$\theta_{t-1}$ --- the frame the closed loop actually presents --- recovered exactly and row-locally
from the segmentation identity
\[
\theta_{t-1} \;=\; \theta_t - \dth_t ,
\]
since \texttt{heading\_rad}$[t]=\theta_t$ and $\dth_t=\mathrm{wrap}(\theta_t-\theta_{t-1})$ by
construction. Being row-local, it is immune to segment boundaries and dropped bouts. No information
is lost for the stimulus bearing either: the absolute bearing recovers as
$\phi + f_{\text{ori}}$ and is then re-referenced.

\paragraph{A residual.} On the few percent of rollout bouts that bounce off the wall, the
post-bounce internal heading is not the chord of the realised displacement, so the train/rollout
identity is only approximate there. This is inherent to any guardrail --- real fish never bounce,
so no training frame exists for the manoeuvre --- and the fix still removes the $40^\circ$ of noise
from the ${\sim}97\%$ of bouts that never touch the wall.

\paragraph{Consequence for generation.} One piece of this change is generation-time and is
described with the rollout protocol (\S\ref{sub:rollout}): the initial heading of a rollout must be
seeded in the same frame, or one trajectory in ten starts facing backwards.


\subsection{Distance-tuned wall channels (the border basis)}
\label{sub:borderbasis}

\paragraph{Idea.} Real fish turn toward the dish centre on $71$--$73\%$ of bouts taken inside the
wall shell (normalised distance-to-border $0.05$--$0.20$), falling to $57\%$ mid-dish. The response
is therefore sharply \emph{tuned to distance}, not monotone in it. The raw border block ---
$[\,d_{\text{norm}},\,\sin\psi,\,\cos\psi\,]$ --- gives the network a single linear knob on that
distance, and what it learns is nearly flat: $0.61$ in the shell falling to $0.56$ mid-dish, a
$10$--$12$ point deficit exactly where the behaviour lives.

The consequences are not subtle. The generated fish heads \emph{outward} on $64\%$ of at-wall
bouts, is $2.4\times$ more likely than a real fish to still be at the wall five bouts later, and
occupies $r/R>0.85$ on $7.8\%$ of bouts against $1.2\%$ real --- including $2.8\%$ beyond
$r/R>0.95$, a region real fish never enter.

\paragraph{How.} Replace the three raw channels with a softmax basis over the covariate, crossed
with the bearing. With $d_{\text{norm}}\in[0,1]$ ($0$ at the wall, $1$ at the centre), knots
$\kappa_j$ and width $w$ in natural-log units,
\[
G_j \;=\; \operatorname{softmax}_j\!\Bigl(-\tfrac12\bigl[(\log d_{\text{norm}}-\log\kappa_j)/w\bigr]^2\Bigr),
\qquad
x_{\text{border}} \;=\; \bigl[\,d_{\text{norm}},\;\{G_j\sin\psi\}_j,\;\{G_j\cos\psi\}_j\,\bigr],
\]
nine channels in place of three. The head can now give the shell and the mid-dish opposite
responses instead of interpolating one slope between them. Two properties are deliberate: the
weights are \emph{normalised} ($\sum_j G_j=1$), so the basis flat-extrapolates rather than decaying
to zero outside the knot range, and the distance enters in log units, so the knots are spaced the
way the behaviour is --- densely near the wall.

This is the same remedy applied to the same shape of defect as the stimulus range basis: a response
that is tuned rather than monotone cannot be represented by one linear covariate, and adding
capacity elsewhere in the network does not fix it, because the bottleneck is the input encoding.

\paragraph{Parameters.} Knots $(0.03,\,0.10,\,0.25,\,0.55)$, width $0.55$, and a floor of $0.005$
before the logarithm ($d_{\text{norm}}$ reaches $0$ exactly). Fixed, not fitted --- they set the
resolution of the basis, and the response \emph{shape} is then learned by likelihood.

\paragraph{Relationship to the frame fix.} The two changes are complementary and neither works
alone. The frame fix removes the attenuation but leaves the model with one linear knob; the basis
adds the knob but, on an $f_{\text{ori}}$-referenced $\psi$, is fitting a corrupted covariate.
Measured: the frame fix alone moved the trajectory AUC by $-0.0015$, i.e.\ nothing, with the radial
features still the dominant importance group; adding the basis on top resolved $-0.0385$
($t=-3.5$).

\subsection{The rollout protocol}
\label{sub:rollout}

\paragraph{What a rollout is.} A generated trajectory is matched to a real one \emph{bout for
bout}, not second for second: it runs for exactly as many bouts as the fish it stands in for. The
stimulus programme is replayed from that fish's record, indexed by bout --- dot position, motion
flag and group size are taken from the data --- while everything about the fish itself is
generated. So the model experiences the true stimulus \emph{sequence} at its own tempo. If it
swims faster than the real fish, it finishes the programme early; that mismatch is real and is
tracked separately as a timing guard, not silently absorbed.

\paragraph{Closed loop.} The position-dependent covariates are recomputed each bout from the
model's own pose: the border channels from its own radius and its own bearing to the centre, and
(when the stimulus is spatial) the bearing and range to the dot from its own position. Only the
turn history and the kinematic history are carried forward internally. This is what makes the
rollout a free run rather than a one-step-ahead prediction, and it is why every defect discussed
in this section compounds instead of averaging out.

\paragraph{Initial pose.} Position is seeded from the real first bout. The initial heading is
seeded with $\theta_{-1}$, recovered from the first bout's $\dth$, \emph{not} with
$f_{\text{ori}}$. This is the generation-time half of the frame fix
(\S\ref{sub:borderbasis}\,--- see the frame subsection): with $10.3\%$ of bouts carrying an
uncorrected $180^\circ$ body-axis flip, seeding on the tracked orientation starts roughly one
rollout in ten pointing backwards, and in a closed loop that error never washes out --- it
determines which wall the trajectory meets first.

\paragraph{Parameters.} None fitted.


\subsection{Interval quantisation}

\paragraph{Idea.} Real inter-bout intervals are not continuous. They are computed as
$\Delta t = \Delta(\text{frame onset})/\mathrm{FPS}$, so every real value is an exact multiple of
$1/30$\,s. The emission is a log-normal mixture and produces values that are not. A classifier does
not need to understand behaviour to exploit that: a random forest separates real from generated
windows on the lattice alone. The interval median has an isolated AUC of $0.95$, which collapses to
$0.46$ once the same feature is passed through a monotone rank transform --- the discriminating
information is in the arithmetic of the values, not in their ordering. It is a measurement
artefact, and it was worth more than any behavioural defect in the model.

\paragraph{How.} Pass the emitted interval through the same measurement operator as the data:
\[
\tau \;\leftarrow\; \frac{\max\bigl(\operatorname{round}(\tau\,\mathrm{FPS}),\,1\bigr)}{\mathrm{FPS}},
\qquad \mathrm{FPS}=30 .
\]
The floor at one frame guards the clipped lower tail from rounding to zero, which would break every
\texttt{finite \& > 0} mask downstream.

Only the \emph{emitted} value is snapped. The kinematic history keeps the continuous value, so the
loop dynamics, the conditional means and the random number stream are byte-identical to the
unsnapped control, and the contrast is attributable to the measurement operator alone.

\paragraph{Status.} This is the same kind of object as the guardrail rather than the same kind as
containment: it is not a behavioural correction and it encodes no claim about fish. It asserts only
that a sample and a datum should be observed through the same instrument before being compared.

\paragraph{Parameters.} None fitted --- the frame rate is a property of the camera.


\subsection{Emission clipping}

\paragraph{Idea.} A log-normal mixture has unbounded support, and a free run draws from it tens of
thousands of times. Rare enormous draws are not merely unrealistic; a single one enters the
kinematic history and drives the loop for several bouts afterwards.

\paragraph{How.} Three guards, all applied to the sample and none to the fitted density.
Travel distance and interval are clipped to $[10^{-3},\,q_{99.9}]$, where the upper bound is the
$99.9$th percentile of the corresponding training pool. The turn is clipped to $\pm180^\circ$,
twice: once on the raw draw, and again after the containment bias is added, so the bias cannot
push a turn past a half-revolution.

One ordering detail is load-bearing. The distance clip is applied to the \emph{average-fish} step,
\emph{before} the per-trajectory vigor factor scales it. Clipping afterwards would squash a
genuinely long-stepping trajectory back under the pooled cap and remove exactly the large steps that
make it long-stepping --- i.e.\ the clip would silently undo the individuality mechanism.

\paragraph{Parameters.} None fitted; the quantiles are read off the training fold. The bound binds
on ${\sim}0.001\%$ of bouts, so it is a guard against divergence rather than a shaping of the
marginal --- which is worth stating explicitly, because the model's remaining interval defect is a
truncated \emph{upper tail}, and it would be natural to suspect this clip of causing it. It does
not.


\subsection{The per-trajectory latent draw}

\paragraph{Idea.} For the models whose individuality is a fitted random effect rather than a
sampler constant, generation must supply a value of the latent. The evaluated fish is held out and
therefore has no fitted embedding --- which is the point, since the claim being tested is that the
model can generate a \emph{new} fish, not reproduce a known one.

\paragraph{How.} One draw per trajectory from the fitted prior, $z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)$ with
$\Sigma$ diagonal, held fixed for the whole rollout and entering the shared body exactly as it does
during training. Every calibrator in this section is deliberately run against \emph{prior} draws
too, not against the fitted per-fish values: the calibrators must see the law that will actually be
evaluated, or they tune one model and ship another.

\paragraph{An implementation constraint that is really a statistical one.} The draw is taken from a
\emph{spawned} random stream, never from the generator driving the emissions. Rollouts are keyed on
(seed, fish, replicate) with no model index, deliberately, so that replicate $r$ of model A and
replicate $r$ of model B are common-random-number paired and the contrast between them is a paired
comparison. Drawing $z$ from the main stream would consume variates the control does not, silently
desynchronising every subsequent emission --- and the paired test would still report a significant
difference while no longer being valid. Spawning bumps a child counter without advancing the parent
stream, so the emission draws stay byte-identical to the control.

\paragraph{Parameters.} The prior is fixed at $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and the between-fish magnitude is
carried by the loadings, because only their product is identified. A single global gain on the
loadings is then fitted by method of moments on training fish, matching the across-fish spread of
the three highest-ICC per-fish statistics: mean $\log$ travel, mean $\log$ interval, and forward
fraction. Note the status carefully: this is one population hyperparameter of a model that remains
a normalised, fully scored density --- not an unscored transform applied after the fact. That is
the distinction from every other mechanism in this section.


\subsection{Two radial mechanisms, both measured inert}

\paragraph{Why report them.} The remaining spatial defect is not the \emph{level} of wall use but
its \emph{ownership}: the population spends about the right amount of time near the wall, but real
fish differ from each other far more than generated ones do (between-fish standard deviation of
mean $r/R$: $0.075$ real against $0.03$--$0.04$ generated). Two generation-time mechanisms were
built to supply that spread. Both measure dead, and the negative result is what motivates fitting
spatial individuality instead.

\paragraph{Multiplicative swing.} A per-trajectory factor $e^{u}$ on the containment bias, the
direct analogue of the turning-style latent, calibrated by a two-point moment solve against the
real between-fish spread and clipped at $|u|\le0.5$ (a $0.61$--$1.65\times$ swing --- a real spatial
personality without letting any trajectory lose containment). On a graded bias the measured slope
is ${\sim}0.0003$ of spread per unit of latent SD. The reason is structural: a bias that is
identically zero across the inner dish cannot move a fish's \emph{mean} radius by being scaled. The
calibrator carries an explicit inertness guard and returns zero rather than saturating the clip ---
saturating was measured to cost ${\sim}0.035$ of mean $r/R$, through the containment re-fit it
forces, to buy ${\sim}{+}0.004$ of spread.

\paragraph{Gate offset.} If scaling the bias cannot work, moving it should: give each trajectory a
personal \emph{home radius} by offsetting the containment gate $u_0$, clipped to $[0.20,0.90]$ so no
trajectory ends up with a bias over the whole dish or with no containment at all. Shifting the gate
from $0.60$ to $0.45$ does move mean $r/R$ by ${\sim}0.03$, so the lever is real in principle. On
the frame-fixed, basis-equipped model it nevertheless probed flat --- $0.0335\to0.0335$ --- and was
not shipped.

\paragraph{What the two null results mean.} Once the shared wall response is correctly
represented, no \emph{generation-time} knob on the containment patch can manufacture per-fish
spatial personality, because the patch it modulates is by then doing very little. Spatial
individuality has to be learned rather than imposed.


\subsection{Mechanisms deliberately absent}

Three further generation-time corrections exist in the codebase and are used by none of the models
reported here. A slow left/right turn-bias oscillator adds turn-\emph{direction} persistence; a
monotone quantile map takes each emitted marginal onto the real one; and a symmetric variance
rescaling about the fixed training log-mean was the rejected predecessor of the innovation
compensation of \S6.6. They are named here so that their absence is legible as a choice: the joint
family was built as a clean slate specifically to test which corrections a properly specified model
still needs, and re-admitting them would have made that question unanswerable.

\paragraph{Two modes.} Reflection is not the only behaviour implemented, and it is not the default.
The legacy mode truncates the step at the wall, and it ships wherever the containment mechanism of
\S6.2 is switched off; enabling containment switches the guardrail to reflection, because the two
were built together. The measured cost of truncation is worth recording, since it is what motivated
reflection: $2.5\%$ of steps are clipped, $59\%$ of those land beyond $r/R=0.95$, and that alone
accounts for the $2.3\%$ of generated bouts in a region real fish never occupy (real maximum
$r/R=0.990$). $28\%$ of all at-wall bouts directly follow a clip.

\paragraph{Instrumentation.} Wall contacts and total reflections are counted and reported per
rollout. This is not bookkeeping. The guardrail's effects are otherwise invisible --- the turn
statistics are shielded from it by construction, and the radial statistics it does perturb are
precisely the ones the containment calibrator afterwards puts back on target. An uninstrumented
quantity that a downstream calibrator can absorb is exactly the shape of trap this project has
already fallen into once, so the bounce rate is carried alongside the metrics as a realism guard.

\paragraph{Parameters.} None fitted. Two constants: a wall standoff of $10^{-3}$ px, and a budget
of four reflections per step, after which the step stops short --- reachable only for a step longer
than several dish diameters, which the emission clip already precludes.

\paragraph{Parameters.} $(c_{\text{bulk}},c_{\text{shell}})$ are chosen by grid search on rollouts
of the training fish, scoring two objectives against their real values: the wall occupancy
$P(u>0.85)$ and the mean radius $\E[u]$. Both errors are expressed in units of the
\emph{between-fish} standard deviation of the corresponding statistic --- the natural scale, since
that is the variation the classifier sees --- and combined with occupancy weighted $2.6{:}1$,
reflecting its share of feature importance ($0.304$ of all remaining detectability for the wall
fraction, against $0.116$ for the mean and median radius together). Normalising matters: a raw
relative error is enormous on a ${\sim}1\%$ occupancy and negligible on a mean near $0.5$, and an
unweighted sum runs the bias to the grid ceiling.

The sample size matters as much as the objective. Wall occupancy is a ${\sim}1\%$ probability whose
per-fish spread is $7$--$12\times$, so a six-fish estimate is not an estimate: it selected a scale
that measures $1.60\times$ real occupancy on all fifteen fish, where the neighbouring grid points
land at $1.02$--$1.12\times$. Since the wall fraction is the single largest surviving tell, that is
the most expensive estimation error in the pipeline.

\paragraph{Three parameterisations.} The form above is the general one; the models in this work use
three restrictions of it, and the choice is consequential rather than cosmetic.

\emph{Tied.} The shipped default fixes the gate at $u_0=0.75$ and ties the two coefficients to a
single scale, $(c_{\text{bulk}},c_{\text{shell}})=s\,(4^\circ,35^\circ)$, searched on a five-point
grid. One free parameter, so it can satisfy one objective. It satisfies occupancy, and pays for it
in the mean: the constant $c_{\text{bulk}}$ acts \emph{everywhere}, dragging mean $r/R$ to $0.385$
against a real $0.449$. That is a defect \emph{manufactured} by the patch --- the untreated model
sits at $0.4525$ --- and it is large enough to make the mean radius one of the largest single tells
in the trajectory classifier.

\emph{Decoupled.} Free the two coefficients and fit them by coordinate descent: bulk against the
mean, shell against the tail, one pass each, since they barely interact. This addresses the
symptom.

\emph{Graded.} Pin $c_{\text{bulk}}$ to zero and open the gate to mid-dish, so the bias is
identically nothing across the inner ${\sim}60\%$ and ramps from there to the wall. This addresses
the cause. The wall excess factorises as entry rate $\times$ dwell time, measured $2.6\times$ and
$2.1\times$ real; a bias that fires only once the fish is already inside the shell is too late,
because the excess is mostly an \emph{entry} problem. A wide graded bias acts on \emph{approach},
while the fish is still heading outward, so it prevents visits rather than shortening them, and
being zero in the centre it leaves the mean alone. It is the only form that improves on \emph{both}
objectives at once: mean $r/R$ $0.419$ ($-0.49$ between-fish SD, against $-0.92$ for the tied form)
with occupancy at $1.08\times$ real (against $1.28\times$). Every point of the other two families
lies on a single trade curve.

\paragraph{A tail guard.} Real fish never cross $r/R=0.95$. A candidate that satisfies both moment
objectives while leaking mass there is manufacturing a feature the data does not contain, so the
search carries a penalty on the generated $P(u>0.95)$ above $0.002$, quadratic in the excess and
weighted four times the occupancy term. It is deliberately \emph{soft}. A hard veto was tried and
misfired: on one widened model no graded candidate could satisfy it, so a lexicographic rule handed
the search to a compliant tied configuration with mean $r/R$ $0.352$ --- far worse on the moments
the classifier actually reads than the candidate it had just rejected over $0.05\%$ of bouts.

\paragraph{Ordering.} Containment is calibrated \emph{last}, and re-calibrated after any mechanism
that widens the model (\S6.4, \S6.6). This is not tidiness. Movement spread drives wall contact,
and the wall fraction is the dominant remaining feature, so a widening mechanism that is added
after the containment fit leaves the occupancy calibrated against a model that no longer exists ---
which is precisely how an earlier variance-restoring treatment lost.

\paragraph{A property worth stating explicitly.} Unlike the guardrail, the containment bias is
added \emph{before} $\dth$ is recorded, so it enters the reported turn, the turn marginal, and the
turn history the network reads on the following bout. It is a closed-loop modification of the
behaviour, not a post-hoc repositioning. Everything awkward about the mechanism follows from this:
it is why the bias must be multiplied rather than offset when it is made individual (\S6.6), why it
is checked against the $|\dth|$ Wasserstein distance, and why removing the need for it --- by fixing
the frame and the border encoding --- is preferable to tuning it.
